## What is the GTM ENEIC?

The Encuesta Nacional de Empleo e Ingresos Continua (ENEIC), Guatemala's Continuous National Employment and Income Survey, is produced by the Instituto Nacional de Estadistica (INE). INE describes ENEIC as a longitudinal survey that builds on the ENEI experience and observes the same households or observational units at multiple times to study employment, income, and labor-market dynamics. The first ENEIC period published by INE is the fourth quarter of 2024.

## What does the GTM ENEIC cover?

The ENEIC collects labor-market and income information along with demographic and other socioeconomic characteristics. Its questionnaires include information on employment, job characteristics, earnings, education and training, and household conditions. Consult the questionnaire for each period because modules and coverage can vary.

The table reports observations in the GLD harmonized outputs. Household and individual counts are household-quarter and person-quarter observations; they are not counts of unique panel members across the year. Expanded population is the sum of the harmonized `weight` variable.

| Year | Quarters | Household observations | Individual observations | Expanded population |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2024 | 1 (Q4) | 13,649 | 51,619 | 17,933,616 |
| 2025 | 4 (Q1-Q4) | 53,200 | 203,676 | 18,079,894 |

The 2024 estimate represents Q4 only. For 2025, the harmonization scales each quarter's expansion factor by that quarter's share of the pooled sample; the annual figure is therefore a pooled weighted total, not the sum of four quarterly population estimates. Because ENEIC is longitudinal, household or person records may recur across quarters.

## Where can the data be found?

INE publishes ENEIC databases, indicators, questionnaires, and supporting documentation on its [official ENEI/ENEIC page](https://www.ine.gob.gt/encuesta-nacional-de-empleo-e-ingresos/). The page provides separate material by year and quarter. Consult INE's release-specific documentation for definitions and access conditions; no copies are included in this CSD folder.

## What is the sampling procedure?

INE describes ENEIC as longitudinal, with the same households or observational units followed at multiple times. The exact sample frame, selection stages, rotation pattern, strata, primary sampling units, and nonresponse adjustments should be taken from the methodology document for the relevant year or quarter. This introduction does not assume that one sampling description applies identically to every release.

## What is the geographic significance level?

The harmonized outputs retain survey-domain information, including Urbano Metropolitano, Resto Urbano, and Rural Nacional. These categories describe survey domains and are not administrative department codes. Users should verify the geographic precision supported by the relevant INE quarter's methodology and results before reporting subgroup estimates; department- or municipality-level significance is not inferred here.

## Other noteworthy aspects

### Longitudinal design and annual counts

ENEIC's longitudinal design means that a household or person can contribute observations in more than one quarter. The annual counts above sum the quarter-level records in the harmonized dataset; they should not be interpreted as unique households or people followed during the year.

### Relationship to ENEI

INE describes ENEIC as building on the ENEI experience, but ENEIC is a distinct continuous longitudinal survey. Its quarter structure, questionnaire, and sample design should be treated as ENEIC-specific rather than assumed to be interchangeable with earlier ENEI rounds.

### Panel overlap and identifiers

The methodology excerpt describes an 80/20 rotating-household design. In the harmonized files from 2024 Q4 through 2025 Q4, adjacent-quarter household-ID overlap is approximately 72-75%. Person-ID consistency checks support the practical use of the identifiers for longitudinal linkage, but are not definitive identity verification. Observed overlap alone does not establish the planned rotation or explain why households are absent between quarters. See the [panel structure and validation note](Panel%20structure%20and%20validation.md) for the diagnostics, limitations, and recommended follow-up checks.
