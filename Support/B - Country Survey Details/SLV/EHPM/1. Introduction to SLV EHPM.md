## What is the EHPM?

The Encuesta de Hogares de Propósitos Múltiples (EHPM), or Multipurpose Household Survey, is an annual household survey conducted in El Salvador by the Oficina Nacional de Estadística y Censos (ONEC), under the Banco Central de Reserva (BCR).

The survey has been conducted since 1975 and provides information on the socioeconomic conditions of Salvadoran households. Its main objective is to support the planning and implementation of public policies and monitor changes in the country's social and economic conditions.

Since December 2022, the survey has been conducted by ONEC. Previously, it was produced by the Dirección General de Estadística y Censos (DIGESTYC).


## What does the EHPM cover?

The EHPM is a multipurpose household survey collecting information on demographic characteristics, education, housing conditions, employment, income, health, remittances, household expenditure, and other socioeconomic characteristics.

The labour module collects information on labour force status, employment characteristics, occupation, industry, working hours, and earnings.

The years and sample sizes of EHPM harmonized for GLD are:

| Year | # of Households | # of Individuals |
| :--- | :-------------- | :--------------- |
| 2021 | 19,627 | 64,937 |
| 2022 | 18,741 | 60,339 |
| 2023 | 17,750 | 56,250 |
| 2024 | 18,058 | 57,366 |
| 2025 | 17,909 | 56,463 |

The harmonized datasets cover the full calendar year, with interviews conducted throughout the year.


## Where can the data be found?

The EHPM microdata are publicly available through the [ONEC data repository](https://onec.bcr.gob.sv/Repositorio_archivos/).

The repository provides downloadable survey datasets from 2010 onward. Access to the downloads requires completing the information form on the website.

The datasets used for the GLD harmonization correspond to the publicly available releases. The updated 2024 release containing geographic information was used for the 2024 harmonization.

Further information, including survey reports, questionnaires, and methodological documentation, is available on the [ONEC EHPM website](https://onec.bcr.gob.sv/encuesta-de-hogares-de-propositos-multiples-ehpm/).


## What is the sampling procedure?

The EHPM uses a probabilistic, stratified, multi-stage cluster sampling design.

The sampling strata are defined by the combination of geographic region and urban/rural area. The survey covers five geographic regions and all 14 departments of El Salvador.

The survey is conducted throughout the calendar year, with the sample distributed across the monthly data collection periods.

The 2025 EHPM followed the same methodological design applied in the 2024 edition. According to the BCR, a revised methodology based on the sampling frame from the 2024 Population and Housing Census is being developed for future editions.

For further details on the sampling design, weighting procedures, and survey methodology, please refer to the annual EHPM methodological documentation.


## What is the geographic significance level?

The EHPM covers the entire country, including all 14 departments and five geographic regions. The survey also distinguishes between urban and rural areas.

The five regions are:

- Occidental
- Central I
- Central II
- Oriental
- Área Metropolitana de San Salvador (AMSS)

The harmonized datasets contain geographic identifiers at both the regional and departmental levels, as well as urban/rural classification.

For the GLD harmonization, the regional level is designated as the subnational level of representativeness. The official EHPM publications also provide selected indicators by department. Users should consult the published coefficients of variation and confidence intervals when producing detailed subnational estimates.


## Other noteworthy aspects

### Survey design and comparability

The EHPM is an annual survey, with data collection conducted throughout the calendar year.

The survey questionnaire is updated annually to respond to changing information needs while maintaining comparability over time. The sampling design is also periodically updated.

The 2025 EHPM followed the methodological design applied in 2024. The BCR has indicated that a new methodology based on the 2024 Population and Housing Census sampling frame will be implemented in future editions. Users should therefore be aware of potential methodological changes in subsequent survey years.


### Household and Person IDs

The source datasets contain household and individual identifiers that can be used to construct the GLD household and person IDs.

The household identifier is based on the source `idboleta` variable, which identifies households within each survey year. The GLD household ID combines `idboleta` with the survey year (`edicion`) to ensure uniqueness across years.

The person identifier is constructed by combining the household ID with `r101`, the individual roster number within the household.

The resulting identifiers are:

- `hhid`: survey year + household identifier (`idboleta`)
- `pid`: household ID + individual roster number (`r101`)

These identifiers allow households and individuals to be uniquely identified within each annual dataset. They should not, however, be interpreted as longitudinal panel identifiers across survey years.


### Survey design variables

The source datasets contain the expansion factor `fac00`, which is used to construct the GLD survey weight.

The `folio` variable identifies the segment and is used as the best available proxy for the Primary Sampling Unit (PSU), as explicit PSU/conglomerado identifiers are not provided in the public-use files.

The `estratoarea` variable identifies the sampling stratum and reflects the region and urban/rural classification.

The source datasets also contain geographic information, including region, department, and urban/rural residence.


### Employment definition and minimum age

The GLD harmonization follows the ICLS-19 employment framework.

The harmonized labour force status is constructed using the source `actpr` variable, which identifies employed, unemployed, and inactive individuals.

The labour module is applied to individuals aged 16 and above. Accordingly, the harmonized `lstatus` variable is set to missing for individuals below this age threshold.

The source employment classification includes own-consumption agricultural activities and other employment categories captured in the survey.


### Employment: Industry and Occupation Classification

The EHPM uses national classifications of economic activities and occupations based on CIIU Rev. 4 and CIUO-08, respectively.

For the GLD harmonization, industry codes are mapped to ISIC Rev. 4, while occupation codes are harmonized to ISCO-08.

The source industry and occupation codes are reviewed against the corresponding international classifications. Where a reliable mapping cannot be established, the harmonized classification is set to missing rather than assigning an unsupported category.

Leading zeros in industry and occupation codes are preserved when converting the classifications to string format.


### Wages

The GLD wage variable, `wage_no_compen`, is constructed using the available cash earnings information for primary employment.

For salaried workers, the harmonization uses `r424`, which records monthly gross cash earnings from dependent employment. Earnings are expressed in US dollars (USD).

The variable excludes in-kind benefits, which are recorded separately in the source dataset.

Unpaid workers are assigned zero earnings. Earnings for self-employed workers and employers are left missing where a comparable cash earnings measure cannot be established.

For further details on the construction of wage variables, refer to the harmonization code and the relevant source documentation.
