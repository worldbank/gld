## What is the BTN LFS?

The Bhutan Labour Force Survey (LFS) is the main household survey used to collect information on labour market activities and employment conditions in Bhutan. The survey is conducted by the National Statistics Bureau (NSB) of Bhutan.

The annual LFS was conducted prior to the introduction of the quarterly survey design in 2023. The GLD harmonization currently covers the annual LFS rounds from 2018 through 2022.

## What does the BTN LFS cover?

The Bhutan LFS collects information on household and individual demographic characteristics, education, labour force status, employment, unemployment, reasons for being outside the labour force, occupation, industry, working hours, and other employment-related characteristics.

The years and sample sizes harmonized for GLD are:

| Year | # of Households | # of Individuals |
| :--- | :-------------- | :--------------- |
| 2018 | 8,855 | 37,113 |
| 2019 | 8,770 | 35,365 |
| 2020 | 8,932 | 35,936 |
| 2021 | 10,076 | 40,830 |
| 2022 | 10,506 | 40,762 |

## Where can the data be found?

The Bhutan LFS is produced by the National Statistics Bureau (NSB) of Bhutan.

Survey reports and supporting documentation are available through the NSB website. Raw microdata used for the GLD harmonization should be accessed through the relevant World Bank / GLD data storage systems.

Users should refer to the documentation stored in the Bhutan utilities or documentation folder for the specific survey year being used.

## What is the sampling procedure?

The Bhutan LFS uses a stratified multi-stage sampling design.

For 2021–2022, the EA code is available as the sampling cluster for urban areas. In rural areas, the corresponding sampling unit is identified through the `clcode` variable, representing the Chiwog or LAP code.

Accordingly, for harmonization purposes:

- Urban observations use the EA variable as the PSU.
- Rural observations use `clcode` as the PSU.

The available harmonized files do not contain a consistently usable PSU variable for all earlier years.

## What is the geographic significance level?

The Bhutan LFS is nationally representative and supports reporting by urban/rural areas and the major administrative/geographic domains used in the official survey design.

Users should consult the official documentation for the relevant year before producing estimates at more disaggregated geographic levels.

## Other noteworthy aspects

### Household and personal identifiers

For 2018–2022, the raw data include both household and personal identifiers.

The household identifier is based on `interview__key` / `interview__id`, while the intra-household personal identifier is `hh_mem_rost__id`.

Identifier availability and naming may vary across years, but both household and individual identification information are available for these annual LFS rounds.

### Occupation classification: BSCO and ISCO

The Bhutan LFS uses the Bhutan Standard Classification of Occupations (BSCO), a national adaptation broadly aligned with ISCO-08.

In the GLD harmonization, occupation codes are treated as ISCO-08 where directly aligned. Bhutan-specific or more detailed national codes that do not correspond directly to valid ISCO-08 unit groups are mapped to the closest valid ISCO-08 category or to the most detailed valid higher-level group where appropriate.

Unknown or unclassifiable codes are set to missing when no supporting label or documentation is available.

A separate note or code comments document the specific BSCO-to-ISCO treatments used in the harmonization.

### Industry classification: BSIC and ISIC

The Bhutan LFS uses the Bhutan Standard Industrial Classification (BSIC), which is aligned with ISIC Rev. 4.

In the GLD harmonization, BSIC industry codes are treated as ISIC Rev. 4 where directly aligned.

Some source files store industry codes numerically, which removes leading zeros from valid four-digit codes. For example, `0111` may appear as `111`, and `0810` may appear as `810`. These codes are converted to four-digit strings during harmonization.

Invalid or ambiguous codes are reviewed using the raw labels and available documentation and are set to missing when a reliable mapping cannot be established.

### Education variables in 2022

The 2022 questionnaire contains multiple education items corresponding to different response universes.

During harmonization, helper variables were created for questionnaire items 2.3, 2.5, 2.13, and 2.15. These source variables are mutually exclusive in the raw data, so the harmonized education variable is constructed by combining the available non-missing responses rather than overwriting one source item with another.

The recode mapping is applied consistently across the four source variables.
