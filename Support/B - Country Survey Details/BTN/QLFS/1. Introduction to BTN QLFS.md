## What is the BTN QLFS?

The Bhutan Quarterly Labour Force Survey (QLFS) is the high-frequency labour force survey conducted by the National Statistics Bureau (NSB) of Bhutan.

Bhutan transitioned from the annual Labour Force Survey to a quarterly survey design beginning in 2023. The change was introduced to provide more frequent information on labour market conditions and to capture seasonal variation in employment and labour force outcomes.

For the GLD harmonization, the QLFS series begins in 2023.

## What does the BTN QLFS cover?

The QLFS collects information on household and individual demographic characteristics, education, labour force status, employment, unemployment, reasons for being outside the labour force, occupation, industry, working hours, and other employment-related characteristics.

The years and sample sizes harmonized for GLD are:

| Year | # of Households | # of Individuals |
| :--- | :-------------- | :--------------- |
| 2023 | 3,008 | 11,398 |
| 2024 | 11,804 | 42,504 |
| 2025 | 11,686 | 41,912 |

Only the Q3 survey round is available in the GLD source file for 2023, which explains the substantially smaller sample size compared with 2024 and 2025. From 2024 onward, the available annual files combine observations collected across all four quarterly rounds.

## Where can the data be found?

The Bhutan QLFS is produced by the National Statistics Bureau (NSB) of Bhutan.

Quarterly and annual Labour Force Survey reports and related documentation are available through the NSB website. Raw microdata used for the GLD harmonization should be accessed through the relevant World Bank / GLD data storage systems.

Users should refer to the documentation stored in the Bhutan utilities or documentation folder for the specific survey year and quarter being used.

## What is the sampling procedure?

The QLFS uses a stratified multi-stage sampling design.

The sampling design was revised with the introduction of the quarterly survey. The available public-use microdata do not contain a variable that can be reliably used to reconstruct the original Primary Sampling Units (PSUs).

For 2023, `psu_str` contains substantially fewer unique values than the number of sampled enumeration areas reported in the survey documentation and therefore should not be interpreted as a PSU identifier.

For 2024–2025, `cstr_code` is also not a PSU identifier. It groups multiple underlying sampling units and should not be used to reconstruct the original PSU structure.

The harmonized variable `strata` captures the first level of geographic stratification using the 24 survey reporting domains in `dcode24` together with the urban/rural classification in `area`:

`strata = group(dcode24 area)`

The survey design also includes a second level of geographic stratification that cannot be reliably identified from the available microdata. The harmonized `strata` variable should therefore be interpreted as an approximation of the original stratification structure for variance estimation.

## What is the geographic significance level?

The QLFS is designed to produce national estimates and subnational estimates across survey reporting domains.

The source variable `dcode24` identifies 24 survey reporting domains consisting of Bhutan's 20 dzongkhags and four separately identified thromdes:

- Phuentsholing Thromde
- Samdrup Jongkhar Thromde
- Gelegphu Thromde
- Thimphu Thromde

For GLD harmonization:

- `subnatid1` identifies the 20 first-level administrative divisions (dzongkhags). The four separately identified thromdes are assigned to their corresponding parent dzongkhags.
- `subnatid2` preserves the distinction between the four separately identified thromdes and the remainder of their parent dzongkhags. For dzongkhags without a separately identified thromde, `subnatid2` corresponds to `subnatid1`.
- `subnatidsurvey` retains the original 24 categories reported in `dcode24`, corresponding to the survey reporting domains.

The four thromdes are assigned to their parent dzongkhags as follows:

| Thromde | Parent Dzongkhag |
|---|---|
| Phuentsholing Thromde | Chhukha |
| Samdrup Jongkhar Thromde | Samdrup Jongkhar |
| Gelegphu Thromde | Sarpang |
| Thimphu Thromde | Thimphu |

Users should take the survey design and sample size into account when producing subnational estimates, particularly for individual quarterly rounds.

## Other noteworthy aspects

### Transition from annual LFS to quarterly QLFS

The QLFS was introduced in 2023 to provide higher-frequency labour market statistics.

This change affects the structure of the raw files, survey identifiers, sampling variables, survey weights, and the treatment of the `wave` variable in the harmonized data.

For QLFS data, `wave` identifies the survey quarter.

The 2023 GLD source file contains only the Q3 survey round. Consequently, the annual sampling weight (`weight`) and quarterly sampling weight (`weight_q`) are identical for 2023.

For 2024 and 2025, the source sampling weights are quarterly expansion weights. In the harmonized annual files:

- `weight_q` retains the original quarterly expansion weight.
- `weight` is constructed by multiplying the quarterly weight by the share of observations from that quarter in the pooled annual dataset.

This adjustment ensures that the pooled annual weights correspond approximately to the national population rather than summing the four quarterly population estimates.

### Household and personal identifiers

The identifier structure differs across QLFS years and quarters.

Starting in 2023, the quarterly raw files contain household-level identification information, but a consistent intra-household person identifier is not always available.

For 2024:

- Q1 includes both `interview__key` and `interview__id`.
- Q2 includes `interview__key`.
- Q3 includes `interview__id`.
- The original Q4 public-use file did not contain a clear household/interview identifier.

NSB confirmed that the unique identifiers were inadvertently omitted from the original Q4 2024 public-use file and indicated that a corrected version containing household identifiers would be shared.

For 2025, a household identifier is available, but no clear source personal identifier is observed.

### Occupation classification: BSCO and ISCO

The QLFS uses the Bhutan Standard Classification of Occupations (BSCO), a national adaptation of ISCO-08.

BSCO is broadly aligned with ISCO-08, but some Bhutan-specific or more detailed national codes do not correspond directly to valid ISCO-08 unit groups.

In the GLD harmonization, directly aligned codes are treated as ISCO-08. Bhutan-specific codes are mapped to the closest valid ISCO-08 category or to the most detailed valid higher-level group where appropriate.

Unknown or unclassifiable codes are set to missing when no reliable supporting information is available.

### Occupation coding: managers and service/sales workers

The occupation series shows a noticeable increase in the share of workers classified as managers in 2023 and 2024, followed by a decline in 2025. The opposite pattern is observed for service and sales workers.

| Year | Managers | Service/sales |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 10.58% | 9.77% |
| 2024 | 11.40% | 8.65% |
| 2025 | 2.63% | 14.83% |

The shift is concentrated among self-employed workers in commerce.

The underlying occupation codes appear to be valid, so the pattern does not appear to result from a standard invalid-code issue. It may instead reflect differences in occupation coding practice, particularly for small shop owners and business operators who may be classified either as managers or as service and sales workers.

### Industry classification: BSIC and ISIC

The QLFS uses the Bhutan Standard Industrial Classification (BSIC), which is aligned with ISIC Rev. 4.

In the GLD harmonization, BSIC codes are treated as ISIC Rev. 4 where directly aligned.

Invalid or ambiguous industry codes are reviewed using source labels and supporting documentation. Where a reliable mapping cannot be established, the code is set to missing.

### Survey design variables

The available QLFS microdata do not permit reliable reconstruction of the original PSU identifiers.

| Year | Available sampling variable | Treatment in GLD |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | `psu_str` | Not used as PSU |
| 2024 | `cstr_code` | Not used as PSU |
| 2025 | `cstr_code` | Not used as PSU |

For all QLFS years, the harmonized `strata` variable captures only the first-level geographic stratification based on `dcode24` and `area`.

The survey design also includes a second level of geographic stratification that cannot be reliably identified from the available microdata. While this limits exact replication of the original sampling design, the first-level strata provide a reasonable approximation for variance estimation.
