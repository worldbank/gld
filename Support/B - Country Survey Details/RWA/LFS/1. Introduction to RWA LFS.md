# Introduction to Rwanda Labor Force Survey (RWA LFS)

## What is the RWA LFS?

The Rwanda Labor Force Survey (LFS) has been conducted by the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda (NISR) since August 2016. Adopting the framework set by the 19th International Conference of Labour Statisticians ("ICLS-19"), the questionnaire is designed to collect information on household roster characteristics, education, employment, primary and secondary economic activities in the past week, previous employment, own-use production of goods and services, and household assets.

Before 2019, the LFS was a bi-annual survey conducted in February and August of the same year to capture seasonal changes in agriculture. Since 2019, the survey has been conducted quarterly, with additional rounds in May and November.

The overall structure of the questionnaire has remained broadly comparable over time, although there are some year-specific changes in individual questions, coding, and the availability of variables in the raw datasets. The May 2020 round used an abridged questionnaire because of the Covid-19 pandemic.

Beginning in the first quarter of 2024, NISR introduced a revised LFS sampling design based on the 2022 Population and Housing Census sampling frame. The redesign increased the sample size and introduced a 2-2-2 panel rotation scheme.


## What does the RWA LFS cover?

The Rwanda LFS is a labor force survey containing information on demographic characteristics, education, and labor market activities over the past 7 days. The harmonized years and sample sizes used in the GLD harmonization are as follows:

## Where can the data be found?

The datasets are accessible to the public through the microdata catalog of the [National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda (NISR)](https://microdata.statistics.gov.rw/index.php/catalog). Researchers need to follow the terms and conditions specified on the website prior to downloading the data.


## What is the sampling procedure?

The Rwanda LFS uses a stratified two-stage sampling design. At the first stage, enumeration areas (EAs) are selected with probability proportional to size. At the second stage, households are selected within sampled EAs.

The survey has used different rotation schemes over time.

In the 2016–2018 rounds, the sample was divided equally into four rotation groups, with one group replaced by a new group in each round. As a result, approximately 75 percent of the sample was retained between consecutive rounds.

Starting in February 2019, the LFS followed a 1-1-1 rotation scheme. Under this design, households in a sampling group were interviewed every other quarter for three interviews before being replaced.

Beginning in Quarter 1 of 2024, NISR introduced a revised sampling design based on the 2022 Population and Housing Census sampling frame. The revised design follows a 2-2-2 rotation scheme: a panel is interviewed for two consecutive quarters, leaves the sample for two quarters, and then returns for another two consecutive quarters before being replaced.

The 2024 redesign also increased the sample size to improve the precision of quarterly national estimates and annual estimates at the district and urban/rural levels.


## What is the significance level?

The harmonized datasets contain a survey weight intended to generate annual estimates. Annual estimates are available at the district level for selected indicators and by urban/rural residence.


## Adjustments during the Covid-19 pandemic

The 2020 Rwanda LFS underwent modifications in light of the Covid-19 pandemic. Phone interviews were conducted for the May and November 2020 rounds, which covered the same rotation groups.

During the May 2020 round, only basic household information and labor status were collected through an abridged version of the survey. As a result, NISR recommends that full-year estimates be calculated only for indicators available consistently across all rounds.


## Other noteworthy aspects

### Definition of concept of employment

Since the adoption of the [Resolution concerning statistics of work, employment and labour underutilization](https://www.ilo.org/global/statistics-and-databases/standards-and-guidelines/resolutions-adopted-by-international-conferences-of-labour-statisticians/WCMS_230304/lang--en/index.htm) in 2013 at the 19th International Conference of Labour Statisticians (ICLS), labor force surveys are potentially subject to a series break because of the change in the concept of employment.

In short, the ICLS-19 resolution restricts employment to *work performed for others in exchange for pay or profit*. Own-use production work, such as subsistence agriculture or construction of housing for own use, is therefore not counted as employment.

The GLD codes the harmonized `lstatus` variable according to the employment concept used in the survey. This has been the case for all harmonized years of the Rwanda LFS. The code can, however, be modified to approximate the earlier employment definition. Further details are provided in a [separate document](Converting%20between%20ICLS%20Definitions.md).


### Household and Person IDs

The source datasets contain household identifiers that can be used to construct the GLD household ID.

For 2022–2025, the annual files contain repeated observations for some households because households can participate in more than one survey round during the year under the LFS rotation design.

In the 2022–2024 raw files, a source `pid` variable is available, but it is not unique within the annual dataset. The same household and person identifiers can therefore appear more than once, reflecting repeated interviews during the year.

The 2025 raw dataset does not contain a source `pid` variable. For 2025, a person identifier was reconstructed conservatively using the order of household visits and demographic characteristics, including sex and date of birth. The matching approach was checked against the 2024 data, for which the original `pid` is available.

Where individuals could not be linked reliably across visits, new person numbers were assigned rather than forcing uncertain matches.


### Quarter and rotation information

Although the Rwanda LFS is conducted quarterly and follows a rotating-panel design, the annual raw datasets available for GLD do not contain a consistently usable quarter, visit, panel, or rotation-group identifier.

This is particularly relevant for the 2022–2025 files. In 2022–2024, repeated values of the source `pid` occur within the annual datasets, which is consistent with households and individuals being interviewed in multiple rounds. However, the identifiers available in the raw annual files are not sufficient to determine reliably which observation belongs to which quarter or rotation visit.

The 2025 file does not contain a source `pid` at all. Although household visits can be distinguished indirectly using the ordering of observations and changes in household-level information such as survey weights, the official quarter or rotation group cannot be reconstructed reliably.

As a result, the GLD harmonization does not construct separate quarter, panel, or rotation-group variables when these cannot be identified directly from the source data.

Users interested in longitudinal or quarter-to-quarter analysis should therefore not interpret the annual GLD person identifier alone as sufficient for reconstructing the official LFS rotation panel.


### Discrepancies between the questionnaire and variables in the raw datasets

While the Rwanda LFS questionnaire remains broadly similar over time, there are instances where variables implied by the questionnaire or survey documentation are not included in the raw datasets available for harmonization.

This includes some questionnaire variables as well as identifiers relevant for distinguishing survey rounds and rotation visits. These discrepancies are documented in [Missing variables.md](Missing%20variables.md).


### Deriving key employment variables

The Rwanda LFS reports contain detailed guidance on the calculation of key employment variables, including labor force status, underemployment, potential labor force, and earnings.

The corresponding harmonization procedures are documented in [Deriving employment variables.md](Deriving%20employment%20variables.md).


### Minimum age for education and labor questions

Education and labor questions are asked only for respondents aged 14 and above.

This differs from many of the published NISR labor market indicators, which are generally reported for the working-age population aged 16 and above. Rwanda labor legislation generally establishes age 16 as the minimum age for employment while allowing certain forms of apprenticeship or light work at younger ages.


### Employment: Industry Classification

The Rwanda LFS follows a customized version of ISIC Rev. 4 known as CISIC (see [here](utilities/RWA_CISIC.pdf)).

In some earlier survey years, source industry codes could not be found directly in either CISIC or ISIC Rev. 4. In such cases, the closest valid higher-level ISIC code was used in the harmonization. For example, industry code `5111` was mapped to `5110`.

For the more recent 2022–2025 files, the raw datasets also contain derived two-digit industry variables that are used where appropriate to construct the harmonized industry classifications.


### Employment: Occupation Classification

The Rwanda LFS follows a customized version of ISCO-08 known as CISCO (see [here](utilities/RWA_CISCO.pdf)).

In some earlier survey years, source occupation codes could not be found directly in either CISCO or ISCO-08. In such cases, the closest valid higher-level ISCO code was used. For example, occupation code `2213` was mapped to `2200` where no valid corresponding lower-level ISCO code was available.

For the more recent 2022–2025 files, the raw datasets also contain derived two-digit occupation variables that are used where appropriate to construct the harmonized occupation classifications.


### Wages

The GLD wage variable, `wage_no_compen`, uses the available information in each survey year to estimate earnings from primary employment.

The underlying source variables used to construct wages vary across years, which results in differences in the amount and type of wage information available in the harmonized series.

In earlier years, wage information may include cash earnings, in-kind payments, net business income, or income ranges where respondents could not provide an exact amount. For example, in 2018 the available data include cash income for respondents aged 16 and above, while in 2019 wage information is not available in the raw dataset.

The more recent files also differ in the way earnings information is provided. For example, the 2024 and 2025 raw datasets contain a derived monthly cash-income variable for employees rather than the full set of detailed wage variables available in some earlier years.

For further details on the construction of `wage_no_compen`, refer to [Deriving employment variables.md](Deriving%20employment%20variables.md).
